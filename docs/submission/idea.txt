Problem: information is hard to sell. To judge a dataset or report, a buyer has to see it, and once they have seen it they have little reason to pay (Arrow's information paradox, 1962). So buyers pay blind or not at all.

Product: Arrow is an inspect-before-pay data market on NEAR mainnet. The seller encrypts a file in the browser and lists it; row data is in no request, and the ciphertext hash, the column names and the row count are public. The buyer's own criteria are sealed to an OutLayer Intel TDX enclave, where a NEAR AI Cloud model that itself runs in a TEE reads a 40-row sample of the data. Only a capped verdict comes back to the buyer: BUY or PASS, optionally fit 1-5 and novelty 1-5, at most 5.68 bits per inspection on the output channel, counted against an on-chain leak budget per listing (run time and instruction count are public side channels). On BUY the data key is sealed to the buyer's key on chain, and the delivered file hashes to the same value the enclave inspected. Agents use the same flow through a CLI, an MCP tool and an HTTP 402 response.

NEAR's role: NEAR AI Cloud gives private inference; OutLayer runs the enclave program and answers the contract through yield and resume; NEAR Intents and 1Click let a buyer pay in USDC from Base; the seller is paid into a NEAR Intents balance and withdraws to Solana in one step, with no NEAR account. If the enclave fails, intents.near's own refund returns the payment to the buyer's NEAR Intents balance, less 10% of an inspection fee.
